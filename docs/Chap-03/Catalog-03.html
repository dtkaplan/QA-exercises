You are to construct additional flavors of one that are conversion factors:

EXAMPLE: Convert nautical miles to inches.

Dividing or multiplying any flavor of one by any others gives as a result still another flavor of one. Flavors of one are conversion factors from the unit on the bottom (denominator) to the unit on the top (numerator). For nautical miles to inches, we want a flavor of one with nautical miles on the bottom and inches on the top. Scanning the list above we identify (c) and (e) as our raw materials. It takes a moment’s thought to figure out whether to multiply or divide here. Division will cancel out the meters leaving the only units as nautical miles and inches. We want nautical miles on the top so divide (e) by (c), giving 72,913.39 inches per nautical mile. As a sanity check on this result, remember that a nautical mile is much longer than an inch, so it takes many inches to constitute a nautical mile.

  1. Convert chains to inches. Divide (d) by (c).

  2. Convert nautical miles to furlongs.Divide (e) by (b).

  3. Convert acres to rod-furlongs. 1 acre is 4 rod-furlongs

  4. (Hard) Convert miles per hour to meters per second.

TipAnswer

On top, we need to convert miles to meters. (f) is the appropriate choice. On bottom, we need to convert hours to seconds, which is (i). So divide (f) by (i) to get the conversion factor: 0.447 mile/hour per meter/sec. Sanity check: 1 mile in an hour is about 1600 meters in an hour which is in turn about 1600/3600 meters in a second.

Exercise 16 ChatGPT disputes that a mole is dimensionless. SI has its own base dimension definition for the “dimensionless dimension”: N. The corresponding base unit in SI is mole, which is a count widely used in chemistry. A mole is 602,214,076,000,000,000,000,000 individuals. When the “individuals” are atoms or molecules, a mole of individuals can be weighed on a kitchen scale; a mole of water molecules weighs 8 grams. In contrast, a mole of people would weigh about 10 times the mass of the Earth; not a convenient unit.

Exercise 20 Turn this into an exercise about the physics of things like seconds-cubed.

  • Velocity, L T-1 where the unit names—e.g. miles per hour, meters per second—serve as reminders of the fundamental dimensions involved.
  • Acceleration, L T-2. You may be aware that “acceleration due to gravity” on the Earth’s surface is approximately 9.8 meters per second per second.
  • Force, M L T-2. Unfortunately, the unit name “pound” gives no clue about the fundamental dimensions involved. And the unit name “kilogram” is not actually for force; it’s simply M
  • Energy, M L2 T-2.
  • Power, M L2 T-3, which is simply energy per unit time.
  • Electrical current is itself a fundamental dimensions, denoted I
  • Electrical voltage amounts to energy per unit electrical charge, that is M L2 T-3 I-1.

In some cases, derived dimensions seem absurd to the untrained mind. After all, what kind of thing could possibly be the result of multiplying volume times surface area, or dividing dollars by time, or multiplying weight times itself? A sensible person might reasonably object: “I know what time is, but there is no such thing as ‘inverse time’ or ‘time squared’ or ‘time cubed.’ But to say”no such thing” is really to mean, “I haven’t encountered any such thing.” Eventually, however, you might. For instance, you will admit that the ages of a random group of people will vary one from another. In statistics, there is need to measure the amount of variation. For this purpose, statisticians have invented a quantity called the “variance.” The variance of ages has dimension T2.
