Exercise 12 Suppose a model is used to predict the effect of an intervention in a real-world situation, for instance, a medical treatment. The model produces a logically correct result, but the model itself leaves out an important feature of the real situation. Which conclusion is most consistent with the chapter?

d01-model-fault
The formal calculation can be correct even though the conclusion is not useful for the real-world situation.       The formal calculation must contain an arithmetic error.       The model must therefore be a fact rather than a representation.       Formal reasoning cannot be used with models.
